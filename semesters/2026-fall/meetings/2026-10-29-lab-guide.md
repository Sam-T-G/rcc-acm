## Arduino track: parking garage gate

Starter page: <https://sam-t-g.github.io/rcc-acm/decks/2026-10-29-gate/> (starter code with a Copy button, the four challenges, answers closed further down).


**Updated 2026-10-28 for the deck:** lights on the top half with resistors to the top - rail, a GND-by-13 jumper, the button in rows 11 and 13, 14 jumpers. Step wording on the slides (arduino_slides.py) is the source of truth.

**Overflow (UNO R4 WiFi, 3 boards):** no sensor left for them. An R4 table builds rounds 1 and 3 plus the challenge 2 button, and uses the button as the car. In the starter, change `bool car = (cm > 0 && cm < CAR_CM);` to `bool car = (digitalRead(2) == LOW);` and add `pinMode(2, INPUT_PULLUP);` in setup(). - a USB-C data cable (the club owns none) **[CHECK]**;

#### Gaps and risks

1. **Sensor pins [CHECK].** The plan stands the sensor up in a20 to a23 with its eyes facing away. Check on one real sensor that its 4 pins point straight down out of the board's edge and fit the holes. If they don't, push 4 female-to-male wires onto the pins and plug the pin ends into a20 to a23 in the same order.
2. **Sensor Vcc and Gnd swapped.** The step says to read the label and put Vcc in a20. From behind, Vcc is on your right. Officers check every sensor before round 2's upload.
3. **Power module left on.** The UNO's 5V feeds the bottom + rail today. With the module still on the board, the UNO's 5V also reaches the module. Round 1 step 1 takes it off. Check every table before round 1 step 3.
4. **Board restarts when the arm moves.** Same trap as 10-15, but there's no battery today. Fix, done by an officer: unplug USB. Put a 100 µF capacitor across the bottom rails, long leg in the + rail (red line), the stripe side in the - rail (blue line). **[CHECK]** that the capacitor's printed voltage is 10 V or more and that this stops the restart on a real kit. Still restarting: try another USB port.
5. **Resistor reach.** Fixed in the plan on 2026-10-28: the lights sit in c5/c6 and c8/c9 and their resistors go from a6 and a9 to the top - rail, the inside rail right next to line a, so no leg crosses a + rail. Check that no resistor leg landed in the top + rail (the outside one, red line).
6. **Readings jump around.** The datasheet says the target should be flat and at least 0.5 m². A hand gives noisy numbers. Bring a notebook or a small box per table. **[CHECK]** that the table under the sensor doesn't give false short readings.
7. **The starter link.** The deck's QR and link go to https://sam-t-g.github.io/rcc-acm/decks/2026-10-29-gate/ (starter plus answers). **[CHECK]** it loads, and copies as plain text, from a phone and a Windows laptop.
8. **Board orientation [CHECK].** Same as 10-15: row 1 on your right, letters upright, line a at the far edge. With the module off, row 1 is the end the module was on.
9. **Real-world claims on slides [CHECK].** Only the claims sourced in design.md section 2 go on a slide. The FULL-sign counting line needs a source first.

### 3. Where the power comes from

**One system today: laptop USB → UNO → its 5V and GND pins.**

- `5V` → bottom + rail → sensor Vcc (through row 20) and servo red.
- `GND` next to 5V → bottom - rail → sensor Gnd (through row 23).
- `GND` next to 13 → top - rail → both light resistors and the button.
- Second `GND` in the power row → servo brown.
- Pins 4 and 5 light the LEDs. Pin 12 starts each sensor reading; pin 11 hears the answer.

The sensor takes 15 mA (HC-SR04 datasheet). One SG90 on the 5V pin is what Elegoo lesson 9 does.

**Never do these:**

- Leave the power module on the breadboard while the UNO's 5V is on a rail.
- Wire the + rail to the - rail with nothing in between. Today that shorts the laptop's USB port.
- Change wires with USB plugged in.
- Put the sensor in with Vcc and Gnd swapped.
- Use 220 Ω on an R4 WiFi's lights.

### 5. Step by step, round by round

**Hole names.** Turn the board with row 1 on your right and the letters upright. Then e21 is the hole in line e and row 21. Rows count up to the left.

#### Round 0: Find your parts (2 min)

The UNO and its USB cable, the breadboard, the ultrasonic sensor, the servo with its arm, one red LED, one green LED, two 220 Ω resistors, one button, 13 jumper wires with a pin on both ends, and a notebook.

#### Round 1: Set up the board

1. **Power module off.** Pull it straight up off the end of the breadboard. It goes in the kit box. USB stays unplugged.
2. **Turn the breadboard:** row 1 on your right, letters upright, line a at the far edge. UNO in front.
3. Jumper **UNO `5V` → bottom + rail** (red line).
4. Jumper **UNO `GND` (next to 5V) → bottom - rail** (blue line).

#### Round 2: Add the sensor

1. **Sensor** in **a20 (Vcc), a21 (Trig), a22 (Echo), a23 (Gnd)**, eyes facing away. Read the label first.
2. Jumper **e20 → bottom + rail.**
3. Jumper **e23 → bottom - rail.**
4. Jumper **e21 → `12`.**
5. Jumper **e22 → `~11`.**
6. Copy the starter from the link on the screen.
7. **File → New Sketch.** Select everything in the new window and paste over it.
8. Plug in USB.
9. **Tools → Board → Arduino AVR Boards → Arduino UNO.** Then **Tools → Port**, the one that says Arduino UNO.
10. Upload.
11. **Tools → Serial Monitor**, 9600 baud. Hold the notebook about 10 cm away: `cm: 10`, give or take.

If it doesn't work:

- **Always 0:** Trig and Echo swapped (e21 should go to 12, e22 to ~11), or the e20 or e23 jumper is in the wrong rail. Nothing within about 5 m also reads 0.
- **Numbers but they jump:** use the notebook, not a hand, and hold it flat to the sensor.
- **Gibberish in the Serial Monitor:** the baud box isn't 9600.

**It works when** (orange slide) the number drops as the notebook comes closer. Talk points while tables catch up:

1. Nothing in front reads 0. Why? The code waits 30 ms for an echo. No echo, no number.
2. Hand versus notebook: the notebook reads steadier. The datasheet says the target should be flat and at least 0.5 square meters.
3. A real gate feels the car's metal with a wire loop under the road. Ours uses sound.

#### Round 3: Arm and lights

1. **Unplug USB.**
2. Push three jumpers into the servo's plug.
3. **Brown → the second `GND`** in the power row.
4. **Red → bottom + rail.**
5. **Orange → `~9`.**
6. Jumper **`GND` next to 13 → top - rail** (blue line, the inside one next to line a), between rows 1 and 12.
7. **Red LED:** long leg **c5**, short leg **c6**.
8. **220 Ω: a6 → top - rail.**
9. Jumper **e5 → `4`.**
10. **Green LED:** long leg **c8**, short leg **c9**.
11. **220 Ω: a9 → top - rail.**
12. Jumper **e8 → `~5`.**
13. Trace every wire, then plug USB in. The arm swings to 0 and both lights stay off, because the starter's YOUR CODE block is still empty.

If it doesn't work:

- **Arm doesn't move:** orange not in ~9, or the red jumper isn't in the + rail.
- **Board restarts, Serial Monitor shows the first lines again:** gap 4.

**It works when** (orange slide) the arm swings to 0 and both lights stay off. Fixes on the slide: orange not in ~9 or the red jumper not in the bottom + rail; a board that keeps restarting gets an officer.

#### Round 4: Make it a gate

1. Under `// If not: red on, green off, arm down.`, type:

   ```cpp
     if (car) {
       digitalWrite(GREEN, HIGH);
       digitalWrite(RED, LOW);
       gate.write(ARM_UP);
     } else {
       digitalWrite(RED, HIGH);
       digitalWrite(GREEN, LOW);
       gate.write(ARM_DOWN);
     }
   ```

   The IDE adds a `}` when you type `{` at the end of a line. Typing the last `}` again leaves an extra one: Verify fails at the bottom, so delete it.
2. Upload. Red light on, arm down.
3. Notebook about 5 cm away: green, arm up. Take it away: red, arm down.

If it doesn't work:

- **Wrong light:** the LED is backwards (long leg in row 5 or 8), or pins 4 and 5 are swapped.
- **Light never comes on:** the resistor isn't in the top - rail, or the GND-by-13 jumper is missing.
- **Arm flutters at the edge:** the notebook sits right at 10 cm. Move it closer.

**It works when** (orange slide) the notebook opens the gate and taking it away closes it. Talk points while tables catch up:

1. What does `car` mean in the code? Closer than 10 cm, and not 0.
2. Slide the notebook slowly past the sensor. When does the arm come down? The moment the sensor stops seeing it. A real car would still be under the arm. That's challenge 1.
3. Change `CAR_CM` to 20 and upload. The gate opens from farther away.

#### Round 5: Challenges (about 14 min)

A round-start slide, the millis() idea slide, one "Pick a challenge" slide with the four tiers below (the first marked Start here; most tables do only that one), and one slide with the 3 button steps for challenge 2:

1. **Unplug USB.** Button over the middle gap, legs in **e11, e13, f11, f13.**
2. Jumper **j13 → `2`.**
3. Jumper **a11 → top - rail**, right above it.

Tables work at their own pace. Officers walk the room. Give a hint before a solution. Show a solution only to a table that has finished that tier or is stuck for good.

### 6. Challenge goals and solutions

All solutions compiled for the UNO and the UNO R4 WiFi on 2026-10-28 (arduino-cli 1.5.1, `arduino:avr` 1.8.8, `arduino:renesas_uno` 1.6.0, Servo 1.3.0). None has run on a board yet.

| Tier | Goal | New idea | Hint to give first | Solution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Car close: green, arm up. No car: red, arm down. | if/else on a sensor | Typed from the slide | `gate_base` |
| 1. Don't drop the arm on the car | Keep the arm up until the car has been gone 3 seconds | `millis()`, the ms since the board started | "Save `millis()` when you see the car. Close when `millis() - lastSeen` reaches 3000. You need a variable that remembers if the gate is open." | `gate_c1_hold` |
| 2. Count the spots | 5 spots. Each opening is one car in. Each button press is one car out. Print `Spots left: N` when it changes | Counting once per change, not once per loop | "Count when the gate goes from closed to open. For the button, count when it reads LOW and it read HIGH last time." | `gate_c2_count` |
| 3. Lot full | At 0 spots, a new car can't open the gate and the red light blinks. A press frees a spot | Combining two conditions | "`(millis() / 250) % 2` flips between 0 and 1 every 250 ms." | `gate_c3_full` |
| Stretch | Arm moves 1 degree every 15 ms with no `delay()`. 4 states: CLOSED, OPENING, OPEN, CLOSING. A car during CLOSING sends the arm back up. Keeps the count and the full light | A state machine: the gate is always in exactly one state, and each state has its own rule for what comes next | "`enum GateState { CLOSED, OPENING, OPEN, CLOSING };` and a `switch` on the state." | `gate_stretch` |

**Why not `delay(3000)` for challenge 1?** During a delay the code does nothing else, so the sensor stops reading. Let a table try it and then pull up a second car.

**Real gates do these too.** A real control arm can be held open by a timer or by a loop in the road until the car clears, and a safety loop under the arm keeps it from coming down on a car (Panish, ConstructionWitness.com; design.md section 2). Challenge 1 is the timer. The stretch is the safety loop.

**Gap in the stretch solution, for strong coders:** a second car that pulls up while the arm is closing goes through without being counted. Ask how they'd fix it.
