Twist three · what they did

Build the ID out of the time

  1. Random IDs?Too big, and they don't sort by time
  2. A separate ID service?Works, but more machines to run
  3. Time, shard, counter, 64 bitsBuilt right inside PostgreSQL
  4. 1,024 IDs per shard, per msAnd they sort by time for free

Source: Instagram Engineering, "Sharding & IDs at Instagram," 2011-09-30 (instagram-engineering.com/sharding-ids-at-instagram-1cf5a71e5a5c). Back then: 25 photos and 90 likes every second. They wanted IDs that sort by time, fit in 64 bits, and add no new moving parts. Rejected: app-made IDs like UUIDs (96 bits or more, some random with no sort), Twitter's Snowflake (needs ZooKeeper and its own servers), Flickr-style ticket databases (a bottleneck or single point of failure). Their answer: several thousand logical shards mapped to far fewer database servers, each shard a Postgres schema, IDs made in PL/pgSQL. 41 bits of milliseconds since a custom epoch (about 41 years), 13 bits of shard ID, 10 bits of sequence mod 1024. Their worked example on screen: Sept 9, 2011 at 5 PM is 1,387,263,000 ms after Jan 1, 2011; user 31341 mod 2000 shards is shard 1341; sequence 5001 mod 1024 is 905. The live counter in the animation is just for show.
