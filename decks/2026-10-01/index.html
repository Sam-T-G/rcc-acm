Twist one · what they did

Make the feed never run out

  1. A feed that never endsInfinite scroll, there is no last post
  2. Ranked, not newest first2016, by interest, relationship, and timeliness
  3. More to watchStories in 2016, Reels in 2020
  4. Posts from strangersExplore makes 90 million predictions a second

Each of these is a whole team: ranking, recommendations, video, infrastructure. Ranked feed and its three signals: Instagram, 2016-03-15, instagram.tumblr.com/post/141107034797/160315-news. Stories: 2016-08-02. Reels: 2020-08-05. Explore narrows 500 candidates to 25 in three passes, 90 million predictions a second (Meta AI, 2019-11-25, ai.meta.com/blog/powered-by-ai-instagrams-explore-recommender-system/). The scores on the posts are made up for the picture.

All of that runs on one guess, what you want to watch next, and now you're the one guessing.

Twist two · build it

Turn watch time into a score

  1. Watched it all? Score goes upSwiped in a second? It goes down
  2. Next reel: the top scoreThe feed learns, one reel at a time
  3. Sometimes show something newOr the feed gets stuck on cats
  4. Instagram tracks watch time too3-second views, swipes, time spent

Beats 1 to 3 are the class's own algorithm, not Instagram's: the numbers are made up for the picture. Beat 4 is real. Meta's system card for the Reels tab (updated 2025-11-11, transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-reels-chaining/): the system gathers reels, a lightweight model narrows them to about 100, models predict what you will find relevant, and it ranks those by a relevance score. Its signals include how many times people watched at least 3 seconds of a reel, how many times it was dismissed, how likely you are to watch more of it than 95% of people who watched reels of the same length, and how much time people spent on it. Mosseri, "Instagram Ranking Explained" (2023-05-31): there is no single algorithm; for Reels, the top signal is your activity with similar content, and it predicts whether you will watch all the way through, like, and reshare. Exploration: ask why a feed that only shows the top score gets boring, and what "1 in 10" trades away.

Watching is half of it. The other half is who you follow.

Twist three · build it

Walk the graph two steps

  1. Start at you, one step outThe accounts you follow
  2. Two steps out: who they followStop here: 300 × 300 is 90,000 already
  3. Rank by mutualsFollowed by more of your people wins
  4. Instagram: gather, rank, filterMutual friends is one of its signals

This is a tree walk (breadth-first search, two levels deep). The 300 × 300 line is arithmetic on an example, not a statistic. Real: Meta's Suggested Accounts system card (updated 2025-03-07, transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-suggested-accounts/): it gathers candidates such as followers of accounts you already follow, popular accounts nearby, and accounts tied to topics you engaged with; ranks them with signals including how many mutual friends you share, whether they follow you, and how many people follow them; then filters out blocked and deactivated accounts. Fun follow-up: Instagram's card says followers of your follows, the class built who your follows follow. What is the difference?

Keep the popular stuff close

  1. Photos load from a server near youA CDN in front of Amazon S3
  2. A cache answers repeat requestsMemcached and Redis, from the start

Beat 2 is the cache: point at the animation, most requests turn around at the cache and only a miss reaches the database. 3 billion monthly users: announced 2025-09-24 (TechCrunch, CNBC). 2011 stack: Django, PostgreSQL, Redis, Memcached on Amazon EC2, photos on S3 and CloudFront, with three engineers and 14 million users (High Scalability, 2011-12-06, summarizing Instagram Engineering). Move off AWS with 20 billion photos: Data Center Knowledge, 2014-06-27. ACM @ RCC

One press per station. Drag to look around; click any beacon to fly to it. Inner ring is classic roles, middle is growing, outer is new. The majors under each title are our judgment of who usually lands there, not a rule.

Pay ladder, how to read it: the bar runs from entry to senior, and the thick mark is the middle. Entry, mid, and senior are the 10th percentile, the median, and the 90th percentile of what people in that BLS occupation earn (May 2025). BLS has no job levels, so say "about" when you read them. A long bar means a high ceiling. Silicon Valley is the San Jose metro. Roles without their own BLS category use the closest one, named on the card.
